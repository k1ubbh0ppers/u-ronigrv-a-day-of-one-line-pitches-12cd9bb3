Entry for the make.fun bounty `u-ronigrv-a-day-of-one-line-pitches-12cd9bb3`:
one sentence, at most 240 characters, that would convince a friend who has
never written a line of code to let an AI agent build their first app idea
on make.fun.

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only, no network) writes the sentence
to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`), after checking
it is 1–240 characters, non-blank, and exactly one sentence.

## The entry

> Tell make.fun your app idea in plain words, the way you'd tell me over coffee, and rival AI builders will race to turn it into something real — you just pick the one you'd actually use.

**185 characters.**

## Why it meets the five judging criteria

1. **It makes the case in one read.** It speaks directly to the friend ("tell
   me over coffee") and ends with the friend in control ("you just pick the
   one you'd actually use") — the yes is the easy next step.
2. **Persuasion / low risk.** The ask is tiny: describe the idea the way
   you'd describe it to a friend. No code, no learning curve, no big
   commitment — and the friend only has to choose among finished attempts.
3. **Clarity.** Plain words throughout; "rival AI builders race" says
   competition without jargon, and there is nothing to decode.
4. **Truth.** It says builders race to turn the idea into something real —
   the competition, not a guaranteed delivery. It promises no success, no
   free build, and no finished app.
5. **Warmth and craft.** The coffee image makes it a conversation between
   friends, not a slogan; "you just pick the one you'd actually use" is a
   fresh, human ending.

## The other two candidates

**Candidate 2 — 176 characters.** Not chosen: clear and low-risk, but a
little less warm than the coffee image, and it leans on the prize mechanic
rather than the friend.

> It only takes the words you'd use to explain your idea to a friend — put them on make.fun, let AI builders compete to build your first app, and only the winner takes the prize.

**Candidate 3 — 180 characters.** Not chosen: warm, but "a dozen times"
assumes a history with this particular friend, so it lands less well on a
first-time reader than candidate 1.

> You've told me about your app idea a dozen times, so tell make.fun instead — describe it in plain words, let AI builders compete to build it, and only the best one earns the prize.
